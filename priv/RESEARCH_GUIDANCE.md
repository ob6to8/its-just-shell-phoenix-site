# Research Curatorial Guidance

Each bullet point should be a **literal association** between the resource and the site's thesis. State what the resource says or demonstrates, and how it connects — supportive or challenging.

## Do

- Describe what the resource actually argues, builds, or demonstrates
- Name specific concepts, patterns, or techniques from the resource
- Flag tensions or open problems the resource surfaces for the thesis
- Let the reader draw their own conclusions

## Don't

- Editorialize ("definitive," "clearest proof," "essential")
- Be defensive or rhetorical ("tell me X is niche")
- Make apocryphal claims not in the source material
- Summarize the resource in general — only the parts that intersect the thesis

## Stance

A bullet can be **supportive** (resource reinforces the thesis), **challenging** (resource complicates or contradicts it), or **adjacent** (resource introduces a concept the thesis needs to account for). Label the relationship through factual description, not by cheerleading or hedging.
